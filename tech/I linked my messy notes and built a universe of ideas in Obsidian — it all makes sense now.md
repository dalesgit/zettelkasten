A good note app is to a writer what a well-worn journal is to a poet. It's something deeply personal.

So, it's only right that we put our tangled thoughts somewhere deserving before they disappear into the noise of the day.

I've always stuck with whatever productivity app came preinstalled on my [mobile devices](https://www.androidpolice.com/best-tablets-for-note-taking/). I started with Google Keep, moved to Samsung Notes, and then to Realme Notes.

Each one was great in its own way, but always missed something.

It was quite the plot twist to use Obsidian with all its complications, although it ticks more boxes than even Google's apps. I've learned to navigate it properly, and I'm never going back to my old apps for many reasons.

## Obsidian is your bedrock of ideas

### It has nothing to do with rocks, but it anchors your thoughts

![Obsidian logo surrounded by connected user icons on a dark background](https://static0.anpoimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/obsidian-logo-surrounded-by-connected-user-icons-on-a-dark-background.jpg?q=49&fit=crop&w=500&dpr=2)

Credit: Lucas Gouveia / Android Police

Contrary to its name, Obsidian is not a mineral guide app about fancy rocks. It's a note-taking app that helps you build a personal knowledge base and visualize your ideas.

It's built on the Markdown (.md) files system, which means drafts exist as simple text files locally on your device. The lightweight format uses symbols for editing, which I'm familiar with on GitHub and most social media apps.

So, expect to use asterisks for bold or underscores for italics frequently. There are also convenient buttons for formatting, so you don't always have to type them.

The real benefit is that my work isn't trapped inside the app. If I delete it, I can still open my files in supported text editors and keep my formatting.

That said, the app looks deceptively simple at first. Opening it reveals a plain workspace with purple icons for navigation and editing.

It's immediately usable, but a learning curve exists with advanced functionality. It's not so steep that it takes days to understand it.

## See inside your head with graph and canvas views

### Finally, I can make sense of my messy ideas

![Note titles linked with lines and nodes in Obsidian graph view](https://static0.anpoimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/google-obsidian-graph-view.jpg?q=49&fit=crop&w=480&dpr=2)

![Notes in square boxes arranged on infinite canvas in Obsidian app](https://static0.anpoimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/google-obsidian-canvas-mode.jpg?q=49&fit=crop&w=480&dpr=2)

Most of Obsidian's settings hide behind menus in the left and right corners. Explore them to use the quick switcher, graph view, daily notes, templates, command palette, and more.

I immediately fell in love with the linking feature. It allows you to connect notes simply by wrapping a title in double brackets.

I was able to relate my poems and papers titled \[\[On Camus' theory of the Absurd\]\] and \[\[Fissures in reality\]\], then jump between them each time I tapped the links. It's among the most realistic features I've ever seen on any app.

Ideas rarely arrive fully formed. Sometimes you only have a sentence, a phrase, or a half-thought. Days or months later, you realize it relates to something else you wrote.

You'd have to copy, paste, or merge those ideas manually, and it'll break your flow. On Obsidian, merging entire files takes seconds. Also, the absence of character limits in the title box was a huge bonus for me. I'm terrible at sticking to one name or short titles.

Another feature I love is the Graph view. You'll see your linked drafts as nodes. Each one represents a note with lines between them showing cross-referencing.

It made me feel like a mastermind piecing clues together on a digital corkboard. Plus, they resemble constellations, which I'm ordinarily fascinated by.

You can customize how they look. Zoom out and see your entire vault from a bird's-eye view, or zoom in to focus on a single note and the ones linked around it.

Adjusting the center force, repel force, and link distance also changes how tightly or loosely the nodes cluster.

If you prefer a compact layout, you can increase the center force to pull everything inward. Personally, I want more breathing space, so I adjust the repel force so that each one drifts farther apart.

It gets even better with the infinite canvas mode, where you can drag individual notes, images, or text blocks onto the screen and color-code them.

It feels similar to working inside Miro or Notion's whiteboard mode. But it's faster and more intentional because everything you add is coming directly from your vault and not uploaded first.

## A space untouched by Google's cloudy hands

### It really is quiet and enjoyable with all those AI gimmicks

![Pop up options for editing and managing opened note in Obsidian app](https://static0.anpoimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/obsidian-note-options.jpg?q=49&fit=crop&w=480&dpr=2)

![Menu showing Core plugins and other settings on Obsidian app](https://static0.anpoimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/obsidian-settings-menu.jpg?q=49&fit=crop&w=480&dpr=2)

Obsidian on Android is one place where I don't miss Google's collaborative and artificial intelligence features. Even if I copy and share a note's URL, it won't open for anyone else unless they already have access to my Vault.

My notes aren't stored in the cloud or synced through a shared server, so external links don't behave like they do on Google Docs. They're merely internal references that make sense within my personal workspace.

I was also pleased to know that syncing is optional, and I didn't have to log in.

The app separates its functionality into Core and Community Plugins.

Core plugins are built in and handle essentials. Canvas, Note Composer, and Page Preview are just a few to mention. Community plugins come from developers and extend what the app can do.

Overall, the app operates on a modular design, so you enable only what you need instead of being flooded with unnecessary features.

Sadly, syncing isn't free.

Obsidian Sync costs a standard $4 monthly for the yearly tier. It's $5 if you pay monthly. Paying is the only official way to keep your Vault encrypted and updated across multiple devices.

## Solid notes take time to form

Obsidian rewards patience. So, take the time to learn the app and [unlock its full power](https://www.androidpolice.com/i-built-a-second-brain-using-obsidian-on-android-here-are-my-top-tips/).

I made the rookie mistake of trying to absorb everything at once and became completely lost in my own Vault. You'll want to start small. Create one Vault, then add a few notes.

Play with the Graph View, and when you're ready, explore Canvas or backlinks. You'll learn faster if you take things one step at a time.

More importantly, allow yourself to be messy because that's what the app is for. It's meant to hold everything that passes through your mind until you can form a sensible pattern from it.
