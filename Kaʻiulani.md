## Kaʻiulani
#quote
#### Princess and heir to the throne of the Kingdom of Hawaii until the overthrow of the Hawaiian monarchy (1875--1899)

**Miriam Likelike**, a princess of the Hawaiian Kingdom and member of the reigning House of Kalākaua, was born on _January 13, 1851_, in Honolulu, Oʻahu. She was the mother of **Princess Kaʻiulani**, the last heir apparent to the Hawaiian throne before the 1893 overthrow of the monarchy. Likelike played a significant role in Hawaiian royal and cultural life, serving as Governor of the island of Hawaii from 1879 to 1880 and being recognized as one of the **Na Lani ʻEhā** (The Heavenly Four) for her contributions to Hawaiian music. She died under mysterious circumstances on _February 2, 1887_, at the age of 36, with rumors suggesting foul play.

**Miriam Likelike -- Quick facts:**
- **Born**: _January 13, 1851_, Honolulu, Oʻahu, Hawaiian Kingdom
- **Died**: _February 2, 1887_, Honolulu, Oʻahu, Territory of Hawaii
- **Mother of**: Princess Kaʻiulani, heir to the Hawaiian throne
- **House**: Kalākaua
- **Notable recognition**: One of the **Na Lani ʻEhā** (The Heavenly Four), honored for enriching Hawaiian music and culture

Likelike's legacy endures through her daughter, Princess Kaʻiulani, whose life and tragic fate are closely tied to the final chapter of Hawaiian monarchy. Her death at 36 remains shrouded in mystery, with speculation of malevolent influence, though no definitive cause has been established.
