Some books never lose their power to move readers.

More than 100 years after **Willa Cather** published “My Ántonia,” two bestselling novelists independently selected the 1918 classic as one of America’s greatest novels when **[Barnes & Noble](http://Barnes%20&%20Noble)** asked contemporary authors to [name their pick](https://www.barnesandnoble.com/blog/our-favorite-authors-great-american-novel-picks) for the Great American Novel.

Novelist **Mary Beth Keane** praised the book’s portrait of immigrant life on the Great Plains, writing that she first encountered it in college after reading authors like **Edith Wharton** and **F. Scott Fitzgerald**.

“In ‘My Ántonia’ I met people I knew,” Keane wrote. “They were ‘Bohemian’ (Czech), Norwegian, Russian. They were homesick, mired in poverty, and suddenly in a place that was alien to them, with only stories of home to comfort them.”

She concluded that the novel’s lasting appeal comes from its deeper message.

“The true heart of the story is about possibility,” Keane wrote. “What’s more American than that?”

Fellow novelist **Patricia Finn** shared an equally personal connection to the novel, recalling that her father first introduced her to it by reading aloud when she was a child.

Years later, she returned to the book with a new appreciation.

“I love it for Nebraska,” Finn wrote. “As much a character in this novel as any of the two-legged ones.”

She added, “I envy those reading it for the first time.”

## Why ‘My Ántonia’ Still Resonates More Than a Century Later

Published in 1918, “My Ántonia” follows orphaned Jim Burden after he moves from Virginia to the Nebraska prairie, where he befriends Ántonia Shimerda, the daughter of Bohemian immigrants. As the two grow up together on the frontier, the novel explores immigration, hardship, [friendship](https://parade.com/1182863/jessicasager/best-friend-songs-about-friendship/) and the promise of the American West through memories that shape both of their lives.

Today, the novel is widely regarded as Cather’s first masterpiece and one of the defining works of American literature. Critics have long praised its lyrical prose and vivid depiction of pioneer life, with **H.L. Mencken** [famously writing](https://www.barnesandnoble.com/w/my-antonia-willa-cather/1116629808?ean=9780143123156), “No [romantic](https://parade.com/936221/marynliles/romantic-love-quotes/) novel ever written in America, by man or woman, is one half so beautiful as ‘My Ántonia.'”

The novel also reflects Cather’s own childhood. Born in Virginia, she moved with her family to Nebraska when she was 9 years old, an experience that profoundly shaped her writing. Many of her best-known novels explored the lives of pioneers and immigrant families who settled on the Great Plains, with landscape becoming as memorable as the characters themselves.

More than a century after its publication, “My Ántonia” continues to find new readers while remaining a touchstone for celebrated writers. The fact that two contemporary novelists independently chose it as one of America’s greatest novels is another reminder that Cather’s portrait of hope, resilience and the immigrant experience still feels remarkably fresh today.
