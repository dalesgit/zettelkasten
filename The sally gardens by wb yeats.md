The sally gardens by wb yeats

**Down by the Salley Gardens** is a poem by Irish literary giant **William Butler Yeats**, first published in 1889 in his collection _The Wanderings of Oisin and Other Poems_. It reflects on lost love and youthful regret, with the speaker recalling a meeting with his beloved in a garden of willow trees--"sally" being an old English word for willow. The poem's central theme is the wisdom of taking love and life gently, as nature unfolds, contrasted with the speaker's youthful impatience.

The poem was set to music by **Herbert Hughes** in 1909 using the traditional Irish tune **"The Maids of Mourne Shore"**, which gave it lasting popularity. This melody is delicate and haunting, evoking a sense of quiet melancholy. The most famous musical arrangement is by **Benjamin Britten**, who included it in his _Folk Song Arrangements_ (1943). Britten's version--sparing, intimate, and emotionally resonant--features voice and piano, with a recurring piano refrain that ends on a poignant D-flat chord.

The poem has inspired numerous interpretations across genres, from classical to folk, with notable performances by artists like **Maura O'Connell**, **Heather Heywood**, and **Kathryn Roberts**. It has also been adapted into hymns, instrumental reels (distinct from the ballad), and even modern song cycles, such as David Leisner's _O Love is the Crooked Thing_
