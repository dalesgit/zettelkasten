**John Donne** is deeply associated with **Ash Wednesday** through his spiritual poetry and final sermon, _Deaths Duell_, which embodies the concept of "holy dying" — a central theme of the Lenten season. His **Holy Sonnet VII**, titled _"At the round earth's imagined corners"_, is frequently read on Ash Wednesday, expressing a profound longing for repentance and divine grace:

> _"But let them sleep, Lord, and me mourn a space; For, if above all these my sins abound, 'Tis late to ask abundance of Thy grace, When we are there. Here on this lowly ground, Teach me how to repent, for that's as good As if Thou hadst seal'd my pardon with Thy blood."_

Donne’s final sermon, delivered while he was dying of cancer, is considered a masterful articulation of **holy dying** — a model of faith and trust in God’s sovereignty even in death. In it, he uses paradox and vivid imagery to confront mortality, urging his congregation to prepare for eternal life through repentance and reliance on God’s mercy.

His works, particularly _Devotions upon Emergent Occasions_ and _Deaths Duell_, are often studied during Lent, especially on Ash Wednesday, as reflections on penitence, transformation, and the hope of resurrection.
