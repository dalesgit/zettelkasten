The ease with which I can get a new note started is pretty cool. I am
almost at the end of *Caring for Words* and I don't want it to end. The
author's so-evident delight with words connects with me in a visceral
and nostalgic place, taking me back to the days I memorized huge swaths
of poetry, was passionate about George Steiner, was in-spired by Eliot
and Yeats, was enthralled with the imaginative force as well as the
message of Kierkegaard, thought language **must be** the key to what it
meant to be human. After an all-too-ordinary 5 decades on, I can see
that much of that, while sophomoric infatuation, provided the foundation
for much of the rest of my life.

I have sometimes shared with conversation partners the sense of being
overwhelmed with responsibility when I realized in Wauwatosa that the
rest of my life would be dependent on the quality of my word craft. I've
been better than some but mostly just ordinary. 

Listening to Mary Chapin Carpenter's *10,000 miles*. Sweet music. I
think it's folk-like that is my favorite music. I could listen to that.
