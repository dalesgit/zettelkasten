# The last piece

The first thing I meet in the kitchen upon waking up, maneuvering to
make the coffee for Mary Pat, is the pie pan in the sink. Thrown in the
sink as a catch all. Sean ate the last piece of the French Silk Pie.
Some time after we went to bed, on his own time, not with us.

And the focus of my bitterness toward is focused on that piece of pie. I
see it as arrogance. Take whatever you can get away with. Sugar, sugar.
No respect. Act like a 10 year old.

The fact that Mary Pat felt like she had to work so hard. The fact that
I had told her several times that it was the best French Silk Pie --
last night I told her it was the best since my mother.

I know it's narrow-minded of me. But I feel it deep in my craw. The cost
of him being here is so great. He doesn't know. #discernment \#
Charlotte 10/8/20

what is at stake in the present time. What I came up with was my place
in the world.

I described the way in which Sean and Mary Pat don't appreciate my
perspective on codependency and addiction. She felt the emotion rise

provided for him. And that we are enabling his dependency.

approaches to discernment; rational, emotional, bodily. Dick helped me
through emotional and bodily discernment. Linda recommended rational.
perhaps the side of me I haven't used enough lately. She suggested
benefits and costs of for example doing nothing or doing something to
establish boundaries?

She articulated the stress response of a number four as being an
unhealthy caregiver. What that looks like is being resentful or distant.

When asked what I want in the moment I described the vision about my
ashes to be spread in Hawaii, my children present, and the beach
alongside Diamondhead where Mary Pat and I were to be married. For her
my place in the world was a suitable phrase for that.

possibly the most important thing she said was that the cost of
resentment is very high in a relationship like a marriage.
