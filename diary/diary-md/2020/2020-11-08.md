# Dream of walking, trying to get home

I was walking and walking. At first trying to catch a bus, then I
realized that I might as well just walk.

Trying to get away from the terminal of some kind -- often in my dreams
-- I ended up in a building. Something like the Notre Dame building I
couldn't escape, but this was different. More like an apartment building
in Honolulu. I was trying to get to a landing in the building that would
let me out onto land. I ended up going all the way to the top. There was
a pre-school that felt a whole lot like **Angels at Play**.

I couldn't get out. I raced downstairs. There was someone behind me. I
couldn't believe they were keeping up. I thought it was one of the
little kids. But towards the end I glanced and it was a man, obviously
chasing me. But he didn't really seem dangerous -- or perhaps I just
wasn't afraid.

There had been a point at which I got to a place that I thought was a
bus stop. But that's when I thought, "I should just walk." Yesterday I
read about someone who, since the pandemic, has been walking 20,000
steps. It seemed like so much. But as I sit more and more with it I
imagine that maybe 3 hours of walking a day would be comforting for me.

There are only two places where I rode the bus very often: Colorado
Springs and Honolulu.

# Walking out of watching TV

I have debated for days what to do when Sean came into my midst without
a shirt on. Walking out. I have wondered whether that was lacking
courage, passive aggressive, not being authentic. And yet I thought
making a big deal about something so seemingly minimal seemed to convey
something I didn't want. Does it convey that I am insignificant? Is my
desire to not rock the boat matter over everything? Or is it that what I
object to his his thumbing his nose? And enforcing some rule doesn't
change that.

Same is true of the dish-washing issue. I don't want to be a part of
Mary Pat's dishwashing crew that she orders around. I don't want to have
to bear with Sean's standards of doing half-assed jobs. I don't want to
have to try to enforce military style rules that don't achieve the goal
of respect and conscientiousness.

Shirley's birthday. Bill and I are agreed that we forgot her.

> For we do not find wholeness until we are willing to enter the broken,
> empty places in our lives and confront them honestly. We do not find
> God's presence without first wading through His absence. *God's Joyful
> Surprise: Finding Yourself Loved* Kindle Edition by Sue Monk Kidd

Reading this tonight I wondered what is my **absence** that I need to
wade through? Emma's rejection? Sean's presence?

> But to grow toward deeper places we must pay attention to our pain and
> its causes and be willing to let them lead us somewhere.

Where's the pain? It's in the loss. But also in the betrayal. In the
loneliness I guess of living in the midst of people who mock what I hold
dear. Reading *God's Joyful Surprise*.
