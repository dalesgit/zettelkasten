# This overview is from [^1^](#fn1){#fnref1 .footnote-ref} {#this-overview-is-from-2}

-   by mid-20th c. countries began relaxing laws, if only to protect the
    mother's life, etc.
-   Lenin legalized for USSR in 1920. Stalin reimposed restrictions
    1936.
-   UK 1967
-   now 98% allow to save mother's life, -- Nicaragua, El Salvador, and
    Vatican are outliers.
-   e.g. 61% in case of rape or incest, fetal impairment 61%
-   abortion (through abortifacients) since 2700 bce in China, 1550 bce
    in Egypt
-   historically particularly harsh punishment for woman acting against
    husband, or slaves performing act on woman of high standing
-   English common law allowed it up through "quickening"
-   a clear pattern was apparent in mid-20^th^ c. that abortions
    continued even when it was strictly illegal -- the question, then
    was "safe" procedures. WHO reports that in places where
    contraceptives are generally unavailable, the rates are similar
    whether legal or illegal [^2^](#fn2){#fnref2 .footnote-ref}
