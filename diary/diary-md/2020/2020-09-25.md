# Singing a morning hymn

The sappy lyrics associated with the morning prayer in *Give us this
day* prompted me to want to write a response.

Morning is a quiet easy rising from slumber. Night feels these days like
a battle to get in enough hours of sleep. I got up once for an hour to
write about what was racing through my mind. Each time the pain in my
bladder gets strong enough, I head into the toilet to try to empty it.
Often it's a struggle. Never is it easy.

The dreams are rarely gentle, although not always a struggle. Children
stolen from my life figure often. Trying to get to where I'm going is a
long-standing theme. Accomplish what I have purposed to do -- is it
someone else's agenda? For many years I was conscious of pursuing the
projection of my father's wishes for me. Being a "doctor". Now? It's not
even the time of life to be pursuing dreams, but, as the director ?
said, waking to read the obituaries to see if I've died.

With Sean in the household it doesn't feel like life restored but no
more life to live.

So waking in the morning is like, "Well, I guess I'm alive another day.
Go make some coffee."

Where would be my joy? How do I sing a prayer or lead-in to prayer.
Recognize the demons? I told Courtney at the prayer group yesterday that
the way she draped the cloth around her, trying to keep warm in her
dorm, she looked like a rabbi in a prayer shawl *(tallit)*. I feel more
like a Jew, rising from the 20th c., staring down the understandable
obscenity of Benjamin Netanyahu's land of Israel, shaking my fist at God
for his often God forsaken promise to choose me for him/herself.

Oh, yes, we rise from slumber, but only to wonder at a world that seems
all too ready to swallow up humanity in a hurry for all the *hubris*
we've thrown up in our million year (or so) human experiment. Sort of
like the several centuries old American experiment that seems set to
come to an end.

We sing, God, but it's a somber song.
