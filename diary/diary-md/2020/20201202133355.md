### Reflect on the relationship between Abrahamic Religions {#reflect-on-the-relationship-between-abrahamicreligions}

Having read and listened to this short introduction to the relationship
and conflicts among the 3 Abrahamic religions, respond to the following
questions.

-   Identify at least 2 things that you have learned that these 3
    religions share.
-   Identify at least 2 things that you have learned that seem to
    condemn the 3 religions to conflict.
-   The epilogue identifies several ways in which these 3 religions
    might find hospitality like 3 strangers before Abraham's tent. At
    the same time it recognizes that there are significant barriers.
    Explain why you do or don't see hope for these 3 "children of
    Abraham."
-   Respond to at least to of your colleagues in a compassionate,
    thoughtful and constructive way.

### Essay questions

------------------------------------------------------------------------

> *From this list of essay questions 5 will be chosen for the exam. Each
> question will be worth 20% of your overall grade for the exam. Your
> response should be two to three paragraphs in length.*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.  Choose one of 2 moral / ethical issues in our contemporary
    society: 1) the availability of legal and safe abortion or 2)
    capital punishment. Explain the difference between **deontological**
    justification of moral issues and **teleological** justification.
    Then give a justification for and against the ethical issue of your
    choice, using both **deontological** and **teleological** arguments.
2.  Describe the 5 pillars of Islam. Briefly explain how each pillar
    exemplifies one of the aspects of religion that you have learned
    this semester.
3.  Outline the life of Abraham as told in the Hebrew Scriptures.
    Describe at least 2 ways in which the Islamic stories and legends of
    Abraham differ from that in the Hebrew Scriptures.
4.  Using examples from the drama **G-d on Trial**, discuss how bringing
    the **beit din** against G-d made a kind of sense to the prisoners
    in the light of G-d's **covenant** with Israel, his Torah which for
    3,500 years breathed life into Judaism, and the threat to the
    survival of the people. Offer an explanation for why the prisoners
    prayed to G-d after finding him guilty.
5.  "I asked myself if it was only the Nazis who had persecuted us. Was
    it not just as wicked for people to look on quietly and without
    protest at human beings enduring such shocking humiliation?"
    (p. 57). Some of the commentators believe that those who were
    following orders were just as guilty as those who gave them; others,
    like Dith Pran, draw a moral line between followers and leaders.
    Would you hold them equally responsible? Why?
6.  "Without forgetting there can be no forgiving," says retired Israeli
    Supreme Court Justice Moshe Bejski (p. 116); the Dalai Lama, on the
    other hand, believes that one must forgive but not necessarily
    forget. Do you think it is possible to forgive and not forget? How
    would you differentiate forgiveness and reconciliation?
7.  We have identified 4 basic responses that religions have made to our
    experience of suffering and evil. Describe and briefly explain, with
    illustrations, the 4 basic responses.
8.  How can ritual go wrong? What happens when it goes right?
9.  What would human life be like without the idea of sacred reality?
10. How does mythology aid humanity in understanding their own lives?
