I am reading *Caring Word* and in particular the section
on \*\*stories. \*\*They are living and vital to human life and to our
particular lives -- they are **life-stories** after all. I was struck by
how my marriage to Bridget ended when I could no longer see or
understand an ending to our **story** together. I said to Dick Hubbard
that, "I can't imagine growing old with her." What I was in touch with
was the notion of her caring for me in a time of dependency or weakness
was repulsive to me. What I just thought in my reading this morning is
that it was about **story**. Our story had ended, I guess. 

I didn't know, at the time, that the story continued with violence and
destruction that I couldn't imagine. In the same way I couldn't imagine
anyone behaving the way Shirley did at Mom's funeral. I have, of course,
come to realize that there are many people who have that relationship to
family. I encountered it the other day in a story prompted by Mary
Trump's exposé on the Trump family. It referenced, e.g., *Mommy Dearest*
and other Hollywood "tell-alls".
