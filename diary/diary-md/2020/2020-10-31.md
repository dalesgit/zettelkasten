# Subiaco

The writer of the reflection in *Give us this day* was from Subiaco
Abbey in Arkansas. I wanted see further about the name. And, of course,
re-discovered that Subiaco was the birth place of Benedict. But also
Gina Lollobrigida and Lucrezia Borgia. She was the daughter of the
cardinal who became Pope Alexander VI . The Borgia's, in her lifetime,
came to their *denoument*  but she apparently survived that part. The
description of her "adventures"  reminded me suddenly of Ivanka Trump.
Lust for power. Privileged life that gave her tremendous opportunities.
Child of a tyrant, probably of a narcissist. I wondered if a near
comparison with the Trumps might be the Borgia's in Renaissance Italy.

# Reflection

The author, from Arkansas, quoted Dorothy Day, looking out at the
terrible things going on in the world (1969) for her and she pondered
what there was to do? Seemingly despair was a reasonable choice. She was
asked by someone, "What would you do?" She replied, "I would keep on
doing what I am doing."

I thought of it in part in the context of Macron's France in the wake of
2 sensational murders made in the name of Allah and because of a cartoon
printed of the prophet Muhammad. Lectures I listened to had to do with
the growth of Rabbinic Judaism and the response of the Trinitarian and
Christology orthodoxies. I had occasion to think again that I felt
myself to be more Jewish than Christian. But I reflected that the
Christian controversies were driven by the constraints of "monotheism."
I wondered that so much violence has been perpetrated, particularly in
France, by Muslims, avenging the "blasphemy" directed at Muhammad. But,
I wondered, isn't that the same thing that Muslims accuse Christians of
having done?

I wonder if there are any Muslim leaders, teachers, mullahs, who are
thinking along the same lines?

I see the world criticizing Macron for taking a hard line against those
who would commit atrocities because some wrote or spoke ironically, even
sarcastically, about the human named Muhammad.
