# Dream on the beach

Last dream of the night I was on a small island. There was a "we" of
uncertain identity. My sense is that Mary Pat was not with the small
group of us but that she wasn't far away. There seemed to be young
people in the group. Perhaps the dream was prompted by the photo I saw
yesterday for the first time that seemed to show Tim & Sue Vail together
with Bridget and the two girls with their beaus, riding in two vehicles
along a beach somewhere.

The photo was clearly not tropical, however. The group of us in the
dream were waiting for a sort of clam back, some sort of sea food broil,
on the beach.

I don't think I was trying to make a long trip somewhere, like so many
of my dreams. Trying to get home from a far away place. I was in an
extension of "home" but not actually home to the place I slept at
nights.

Then I woke, deciding to pee. It was only the 2nd time of the and it
felt good not waking every 1½ hours. But it meant that I was going to
have to get up without even a full 6 hours of sleep. Mary Pat's alarm
would go off in a few minutes, I couldn't fall back asleep. So here I
am.

And I wonder about how putting these few words together, forming
sentences, fashioning the meaning on the page to correspond in some
sense with the narrative inside of me, trying to locate the meanings
that have some significance, these things give me joy.

Not less, I suppose, than sewing the place mats that Mary Pat finished
needle pointing over 20 years ago. Than a small bag I'd like to make to
hold my sunglasses.

# Ignorance and misinformation about the holocaust

After watching the movie *Seventh Cross* (1944), Mary Pat googled the
question. She read aloud startling data and surveys that show a stunning
lack of awareness and even great inclination to believe misinformation
in the US. She seemed surprised by the information that "some countries
have made it illegal" to teach or proclaim a denial of the holocaust. In
the US, she reported, some of the states that don't mandate education on
Holocaust have populations with a higher awareness of the truth than in
other states, like South Carolina, Texas, Florida, that do require
teaching about the holocaust.

I'm interested in the whole misinformation thing, the abandonment of
belief in "truth", the question of whether or not it's true that denying
false information gives rise to a greater conviction of the
mis-information -- this I have repeated after hearing a story on
HIV/AIDS in the early 2000's while driving on Kalanianaole HWY.

Yesterday I listened to the last (32nd) lecture by Philip Carey I think
his name is, from Eastern Pennsylvania Univ., on Philosophy and
Religion. He made it much clearer his own position as a protestant
reformed trained theologian. He spoke of George Lindbeck in the same
lecture as some Reformed philosophers, crafting an alternative
post-modern epistemology. His protestant background left me cold, but he
did prompt a lot of reflection on "truth and knowledge". His summary of
Lindbeck seemed warmly familiar to me, sounding a kinship with
anthropology as I, too, have understood for many years. Carey felt a
much greater alignment with "history" and it felt so **tired**to me.

But that's the background for the bewildering abandonment of trust and
belief in truth that now plagues our society. In part it is the
transformation that has happened in our students, as our educational
system for 2 generations or more has emphasized diversity, together with
an awareness of how different are the experiences (inner at least) of
people from different backgrounds and cultures. What the take-away is,
though, of these students, getting a shallow understanding, is a belief
in the relativity of truth. It is whatever an individual believes it to
be. That is no truth at all.
