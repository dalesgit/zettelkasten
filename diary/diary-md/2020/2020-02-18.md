Every grading system that I know of that seeks to integrate percentages
and letter grades has to deal with trade-offs in the formulae that do
the work. One such in the system I use in grading exams occurs when one
puts a number on answers for which the student has not provided any
answer. It has seemed to me to be unfair that the grade one gives an
answer that is deficient would have the same numeric value as where the
student doesn\'t even make an attempt to answer. However, in
conversations with students after our first exam, I have decided to
change the formula I use for grading the essays \-- at least for this
exam. Questions that were left blank will receive the numeric value of
60% (on a 1-100 scale).

The result is that the final grade for a good many of you will be higher
than what was on the note attached to your exam. No one\'s grade is
lowered by changing the formula. You can see your revised grade by
looking at BlackBoard.
