# Subjective Truth {#Subjective Truth}

Reading today from Mary history of mathematics, I read two paragraphs
describing George Berkeley's sweeping critique of the leading
mathematicians of his day, Newton and Leibniz. He was focused on their
separate but contemporary understanding of calculus. He made the case
that they were nowhere near as absolute in their knowledge as they
portrayed, but rather they fudged in all kinds of places where they
claimed certainty. I thought immediately of the chapters I have read
about him and his role in forging radical skepticism, in the midst of
the 18th century.

Mary Pat invited me to read these pages in the light of my sharing with
her my experience is the last 24 hours with my students, as I attempted
to address the radical relativism of their thought processes. I learned
from one student that in their hmxp class the teacher had advocated for
alternate truths. A phrase for truth as subjectivity to use a phrase of
Kierkegaard's. The best student in my morning class seemed surprised to
realize that I didn't regard religion as an opinion but rather as some
higher kind of epistemological knowledge.

In a corset today, perhaps in the shower, I determined on a response to
Berkeley's critique. it is reminiscent of Kierkegaard's phrase:

> we only understand backwards oh, but we have to live forwards.

This is of course implies that we live forward into uncertainty, into a
gray world when what seems looking backwards to be black and white.

This leads me to this formulation:

> we can know nothing with absolute certainty, but we know a great many
things, far too many even to catalog, with approximation. The relativity
that we live in is like the relativity of Chaos Theory. It is founded on
the theoretical absolute certainty that we cannot know, but it reliably
predicts that small shifts can have a major impact on systems. Our
knowing things is a system. We cannot know the time and the place for an
electron in an absolute sense, but we know approximately. (W.
Heisenberg)
