Yesterday was Sunday. Preaching at St. Peter's. I got part of the way
through my text which had gone through at least 3 drafts and there was a
part that I had not revised. I was panicky inside, thinking that the
version that had downloaded onto the iPad for delivery's sake was
corrupted. I was scared to follow the text from then on. I felt hampered
and constricted. The text was Amos and Luke 8.

Later Mary Pat (at Barb's) was concerned with Mary Magdalene, Mary
Bethany, and sinful woman anointing Jesus' feet -- all of them
conflated in the western tradition until Paul VI -- Mary Magdalene
appears also in Luke 8.

It turned out, as Cindy said at St. Peter's, that the best part of the
day for her was my spontaneous proclamation after we finished mass, that
the dismissal at St. Peter's is the best I had experienced at any
church in the diocese, large or small. When we announced, "Let us go
forth in the name of Christ" that's what we did. Every other place it
was muddied by my struggle to manipulate my words of dismissal to
actually be the last words of the liturgy.

Cindy appreciated that, I think, as a concrete illustration of my claim
that Jesus' admonition to us was directed at large or small churches,
but was to do one thing and to do it well, with love. (I could have
quoted the song from Donovan that is a part of my monthly readings from
Northumbria.) As it was I quoted Judy Small's "One Voice" which Mary
Pat liked.
