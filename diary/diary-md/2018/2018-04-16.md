# Dream: last week of class {#Dream: last week of class}

Through several cycles of waking before getting out of bed. Even going
to pee once. There were dreams that had familiar themes going on and a
familiar face. The face was Betty Spillman. The setting was some kind of
conference or training camp. We were housed in a maze like giant
institution that I have identified in the past as Notre Dame. I was
trying to be with Betty, but was stymied at every turn. Where was Mary
Pat? The whole thing seemed more like a symbolic fable than a reality
based story.

## Talk with Bill {#Talk with Bill}

He called because he had been depressed. Phone call with Glick this
morning led to him deciding to put Mom back in the hospital. Her kidneys
and liver are not functioning properly. We both talked about organs
shutting down. But Glick said to him that he was going to recommend that
we \"try one more time\" to get Mom out of what has dragged her down.

I don\'t really think that Mom would have made the decision Bill did,
but I whole-heartedly support Bill in making the decision. I told him
that Glick is the one medical professional I would trust to have Mom\'s
best interests in mind. I told Bill that Glick can make the call for
hospice, he did it with George. Bill agreed.

Bill put the issue for him as, \"I don\'t know where I should be.\" I
contrasted that with having things to []{#do}**do**. I asked if he would
feel bad if he\'s in Flagstaff when she died. He said not any worse than
being in Las Cruces. He understands that Karla is a strength and support
for him. I recognizes that he is sick and needs to get well.

Mary Pat and I see the possibility of needing to be in Las Cruces after
finals are over. It seems that it won\'t be that long. I said to her
that it wasn\'t that I don\'t have any desire to be with Mom. It is that
I would gladly choose, if I could, to be with Mom when she dies. I
recognize it as an event that is intensely intimate and blessed. But I
recognize that all too often we don\'t get to choose who will be with
someone when they die. Charlene has a better chance. But there\'s no
guarantee there.

Shalom, Mom. Blessings. Mercy. And laugh along the way.
