established himself as a well respected professor whom colleagues and

courses per semester, two sections of our Introduction to Religious
Studies (RELG 101) and one other, which varies. This year, he had four
sections of 101 (with a coupld of attached Honors sections) and Intro to
Hebrew Bible / Old Testament (RELG 313) in fall and Modern Christian
Thought (RELG 317) in spring.

I can say from observation of his office presence, discussion with him,
and review of his course syllabi and other materials that Dale pours
himself whole heartedly into his teaching. This is equally reflected now
in his reporting on each of his courses and his reflections on student
evaluations and student learning. For each course, he gives a review of
what he aims to accomplish, the changes he has made to his materials and
delivery, what worked well and what not so well, and the student
reactions. Dale definitely cares about his students beyond their simply
succeeding in his course, but their intellectual development, and their
ability to read, think, and write critically and intelligently in our
field of religions, religious studies, and theology.

Student evaluations are indeed generally quite positive. We must work,
however, to find ways to improve student response to the online course
evaluation. Of all six courses Dale taught, the response rates were 68%,
67%, 61%, 52%, 41%, and 38%. this has to be better.

It is also of some interest that in each of these sections, there are
always students who express their dissatisfaction with the clarity of
Dale\'s expectations. They are quite clearly a minority, but this may be
something to work on in the future.

In his summary, Dale mentions having published some of his professional
writings. As an adjunct, he is not required to maintain a scholarly
profile, but it would be interesting for him to list some of his work in
the Scholarly Activity section of the report so that we can have an
appreciation of his writing and presentations.

Another very good year for Dale Hathaway. We are grateful to have him
teaching on a regular basis for us, especially since a number of our
full-timers are serving in administrative roles and so are not teaching
their usual load. Dale is an asset to the department in both skill and
attitude.
