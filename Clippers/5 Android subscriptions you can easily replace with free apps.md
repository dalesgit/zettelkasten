I pay monthly subscriptions for a bunch of apps. Some, like Adobe's Lightroom mobile, are genuinely worth the cost for how effortless it makes photo enhancements. None of the alternatives even come close to its combination of intuitiveness, speed, and sophistication.

Other paid apps, not quite so much. [I use Syncthing to sync Obsidian files across devices](https://www.makeuseof.com/sync-your-files-for-free-with-syncthing-on-all-devices/), Immich as a replacement for Google Photos, OSS Document Scanner as an alternative to CamScanner, and NewPipe to fix the annoyances of YouTube. These free apps can easily replace their paid counterparts and save you the subscription cost.

## Syncthing

### Device-to-device syncing without cloud fees

-   ![SyncTrayzor 2 GUI authentication page](https://static0.makeuseofimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/wm/2025/11/synctrayzor-2-gui-authentication-page.png?q=49&fit=contain&w=750&h=422&dpr=2)

[Obsidian is by far my favorite note-taking app](https://www.makeuseof.com/stop-switching-note-apps-choose-obsidian/), but it doesn't offer free sync. Obsidian Sync costs $4 a month, which is well worth it if you use the app extensively. However, I'm still thinking about a long-term commitment, so I needed a way to sync my notes across my devices without the recurring cost.

Syncthing solved this problem for me. It's an open-source peer-to-peer tool that creates direct links between my devices instead of routing everything through a cloud server. My notes sync almost instantly over my home Wi-Fi, and the data never leaves my network. The initial setup takes some effort because the official Syncthing clients are not in active development. I use SyncTrayzor on Windows and Syncthing-Fork on Android, but once configured, it runs quietly in the background. I've been using it for months now without any issues.

Syncthing is also more flexible than any official solutions. I can set up two-way sync for my active projects and one-way sync for archived files, saving storage on my phone. It's not as seamless as a one-click subscription service, but the trade-off is complete control over my data and no monthly fees.

## Immich

### Self-hosted Google Photos that's faster

   ![Using Immich for photo backups](https://static0.makeuseofimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/wm/2025/12/using-immich-for-photo-backups-1.jpg?q=49&fit=crop&w=825&dpr=2)

Google Photos works fine until you hit the storage limit. Then you're looking at Google One subscriptions or losing access to the smart features that made it worthwhile in the first place. [I host my own Google Photos alternative with Immich](https://www.makeuseof.com/self-host-immich-google-photos-alternative-faster/), and it's turned out to be faster than Google's offering.

Immich is a self-hosted photo backup and management system that runs on your own hardware. I set it up on a Linux Mint server using Docker, though it also works on Windows and macOS. With my roughly 150GB photo library, Immich feels noticeably snappier than Google Photos and offers instant thumbnails, smooth timeline scrubbing, and no lag when opening full-resolution images.

The catch is that you're responsible for backups and maintenance. There's no safety net if your hardware fails, so you need a separate backup solution like Duplicati.

   ![immich](https://static0.makeuseofimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/immich.png?q=70&fit=contain&w=420&dpr=1)

##### Immich

**OS**

Linux, Windows, macOS, Android, iOS

**Price model**

Free

Immich is a self-hosted solution that allows you back up, organize, and manage your photos on your own server. It allows you to browse your photos and videos with ease and does not sacrifice privacy.

## Bitwarden

### Free password management across all devices

    ![Bitwarden app showing self-hosted option.](https://static0.makeuseofimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/wm/2026/01/bitwarden-app-showing-self-hosted-option.JPG?q=49&fit=crop&w=825&dpr=2)<small>Credit:&nbsp;Yadullah Abidi / MakeUseOf</small>

For the longest time, I relied on my browser's password manager to store my credentials online. It worked fine until [I switched to passkeys](https://www.makeuseof.com/why-passkeys-are-better-than-passwords/) and needed a way to sync them across devices, which the browser managers lack. [Instead of paying for a password manager, I opted for Bitwarden](https://www.makeuseof.com/dont-need-password-manager-subscription-bitwarden-free-is-perfect/).

Switching was painless, too. Bitwarden's import feature pulled all my saved passwords from Chrome in seconds, and everything synced automatically across my phone, laptop, and browser extension. The premium tier costs about $1 a month if you want extras like the integrated authenticator, but the free version covers everything most people need.

   ![bitwarden logo](https://static0.makeuseofimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/bitwarden-logo.png?q=70&fit=contain&w=420&dpr=1)

##### Bitwarden

**OS**

Cross-platform

**Price model**

Free, Premium available

Bitwarden is a secure, open-source password manager that helps you generate, store, and autofill strong passwords across all your devices. It uses end-to-end encryption, meaning only you can access your data—not even Bitwarden itself. With support for passkeys, secure notes, and cross-platform apps, it’s a privacy-focused alternative to built-in browser password managers.

## OSS Document Scanner

### Scan documents without ads or subscriptions

    ![OSS Document Scanner kept on a Laptop ](https://static0.makeuseofimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/wm/2026/01/oss-document-scanner-kept-on-a-laptop.jpg?q=49&fit=crop&w=825&dpr=2)<small>Credit:&nbsp;Tashreef Shareef / MakeUseOf</small>

CamScanner is the most popular document scanner app, but for occasional use, paying for the premium version to remove ads feels excessive. Instead, I use OSS Document Scanner, an open-source and completely free alternative that matches the polish of paid apps.

Because it's open source, the code is publicly auditable. When you're scanning IDs, contracts, and tax documents, knowing that nothing is being processed by a third-party server is important for privacy. The app is free to download and available on the Play Store, and it's become my go-to recommendation for anyone scanning documents on Android.

   ![OSS Document Scanner logo](https://static0.makeuseofimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/oss-document-scanner-logo.png?q=70&fit=contain&w=420&dpr=1)

##### OSS Document Scanner

**OS**

Android

**Pricing model**

Free

OSS Document Scanner is a free, open-source Android scanner that captures documents via camera or import, auto-detects and crops them, and creates shareable PDFs with OCR.

## NewPipe

### YouTube without ads or tracking

NewPipe lets you play audio in the background, use picture-in-picture mode, and download videos for offline viewing. The trade-off is that you can't sign in with your Google account, so you lose your subscriptions and recommendations. But you can manually add channels within NewPipe and build a curated feed without the algorithm pushing content at you.

It's also not on the Play Store, so you'll need to sideload the APK or install it through F-Droid. That said, for anyone tired of YouTube's aggressive monetization, it's worth the minor inconvenience.

   ![NewPipe icon](https://static0.makeuseofimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/newpipe-icon.png?q=70&fit=contain&w=420&dpr=1)

Watch and download videos with NewPipe, a lightweight YouTube alternative for Android. Enjoy ad-free playback, background listening, and privacy-focused features.

### Apps worth trying before subscribing

Not every free alternative matches the polish of paid subscriptions. Some require more setup, others demand ongoing maintenance, and a few trade convenience for control. But for tools you use occasionally or services where you're paying for features you could get elsewhere, these apps prove that a monthly fee isn't always necessary. Apart from saving you on monthly subscription costs, you might prefer the extra control over your data anyway.
