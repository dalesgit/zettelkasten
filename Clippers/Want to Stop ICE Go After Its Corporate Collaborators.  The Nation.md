# Want to Stop ICE? Go After Its Corporate Collaborators. | The Nation

> ## Excerpt
> ICE can’t function without help from the private sector. So we should force the private sector to stop helping.

---
[Activism](https://www.thenation.com/subject/activism/) / January 9, 2026

ICE can’t function without help from the private sector. So we should force the private sector to stop helping.

![Marchers in Charlotte, North Carolina protest the ICE killing of Renee Nicole Good, on January 8, 2026.](https://www.thenation.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/GettyImages-2254733987.jpg)

Marchers in Charlotte, North Carolina, protest the ICE killing of Renee Nicole Good, on January 8, 2026.

(Peter Zay / Anadolu via Getty Images)

Renee Nicole Good’s [murder](https://www.thenation.com/article/society/minneapolis-renee-nicole-good-vigil/) by an ICE agent in Minneapolis has left millions of Americans wondering how we can stop ICE from terrorizing our communities any further. There are many well-known ICE-fighting tactics that we can and should use, like protests, know-your-rights trainings, and neighborhood watches. But two recent victories show a promising, relatively underutilized path forward—one that deserves to be pursued further: We can target businesses that work with ICE.

ICE relies heavily on the private sector to help carry out its Gestapo-like crusade against immigrants and their allies. Without the logistical, financial, and political support of business, its capacity to terrorize our communities would crumble.

-   **AT&T** ($83 million IT and network [contract](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_70CTD021FR0000018_7012_GS00Q17NSD3000_4732) with ICE, with a potential end date of July 2032)
-   **LexisNexis** ($21 million data-brokerage [contract](https://lawreview.colorado.edu/print/volume-95/lexisnexiss-contract-with-ice-as-unjust-enrichment-lizzie-bird/) with ICE. This company is particularly vulnerable to pressure from university students and professor unions, since much of its revenue comes from colleges.)
-   **Home Depot and Lowe’s** are [using](https://www.404media.co/home-depot-and-lowes-share-data-from-hundreds-of-ai-cameras-with-cops/) AI-powered license plate readers and feeding this data into law enforcement surveillance systems accessible to ICE. Their parking lots are also [regular sites](https://www.cnn.com/2025/07/18/business/home-depot-ice-day-laborers) of ICE raids targeting day laborers.

-   **Amazon** [provides](https://www.immigrantdefenseproject.org/wp-content/uploads/How-Amazon-Powers-ICEs-Deportation-Machine.pdf) ICE with the digital backbone for its data and surveillance operations through Amazon Web Services. Amazon’s Whole Foods stores are a rich potential target for nonviolent disruption on big days of action.
-   **Palantir** [provides](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202509/palantir-ice-and-the-quiet-expansion-of-a-biometric-dragnet) ICE with core data platforms that integrate and analyze information from many databases so agents can search, link, and manage deportation operations.
