##### _The Quaker painter Edward Hicks (1789-1849) created this iconic image of “[The Peaceable Kingdom](https://substack.com/redirect/71938923-c9ea-4ece-8932-e30228094156?j=eyJ1IjoiMW91ejZvIn0.xCUUX-G1mqOe_p0uPEybRD6sQoY3OtX97OlEZbEJxWg),” a vision of peace on earth based on Isaiah 11:6-9: "The wolf will live with the lamb, the leopard will lie down with the goat, the calf and the lion and the yearling together; and a little child will lead them." The painting is a “visual sermon” to remind Quakers of their ideals._

**I. I’m a Quaker, a member of the [Religious Society of Friends](https://substack.com/redirect/6fcce19a-3b29-443c-b4f9-630bf2dd2a55?j=eyJ1IjoiMW91ejZvIn0.xCUUX-G1mqOe_p0uPEybRD6sQoY3OtX97OlEZbEJxWg).** Most people don’t know much about us—except for the oatmeal we don’t make—so here’s a primer: Quakerism began as a mid-17th century protest against what its founders saw as the Church of England’s distortion of the Gospel. The branch of Quakerism I belong to has no clergy and no creed, no list of fixed beliefs one has to sign to join, and does not believe that the Spirit stopped speaking when the Holy Writ got locked down. We believe in “continuing revelation,” in the human capacity to hear and act on the imperatives of the Spirit, and in living nonviolently at every level of our lives.

> **In place of a creed, Quakers aspire** to live by [Testimonies](https://substack.com/redirect/a635cb06-f52f-475f-80e7-6b9781ded4eb?j=eyJ1IjoiMW91ejZvIn0.xCUUX-G1mqOe_p0uPEybRD6sQoY3OtX97OlEZbEJxWg) that reflect what we have learned about the values we’re called to embody: equality, peace and social justice, integrity, simplicity, community, and care for the earth. We often fall short of our aspirations, but our Testimonies have found expression in various real-world ways.

> **Quakers were the first American religious community** to oppose slavery, making ownership of enslaved people grounds for expulsion ninety years before the Civil War. Some have expressed their opposition to war by becoming conscientious objectors and [war tax resisters](https://substack.com/redirect/1c964567-656e-448f-88cf-bbd90d2bff09?j=eyJ1IjoiMW91ejZvIn0.xCUUX-G1mqOe_p0uPEybRD6sQoY3OtX97OlEZbEJxWg)—though COs have served in unarmed military roles (e.g., [battlefield medics](https://substack.com/redirect/75605c09-d5a6-4898-b00a-9681e8975d70?j=eyJ1IjoiMW91ejZvIn0.xCUUX-G1mqOe_p0uPEybRD6sQoY3OtX97OlEZbEJxWg)), and war tax resisters normally give to nonprofits what they withhold from the IRS. Though small in number (around 80,000 in the U.S. today), Quakers have had a disproportion presence in nonviolent struggles against war and for racial justice, as well as relief and rebuilding efforts in war-torn territories. Across the U.S., Quakers have had a strong presence in [education](https://substack.com/redirect/d5951939-bcd2-4a49-9c1e-f77dc1d3edf6?j=eyJ1IjoiMW91ejZvIn0.xCUUX-G1mqOe_p0uPEybRD6sQoY3OtX97OlEZbEJxWg).

**II. I discovered Quakerism when I was in my mid-thirties,** and quickly found it to be the spiritual home I’d been looking for. It made sense to me that every human being has direct access to the Spirit—that the first place to go when I’m in need of guidance is not to a book or an expert, but to what Quakers call “that of God within us,” an indwelling voice of love, truth and justice.

> **But given what I know about my own capacity for self-deception,** it also made sense that I would need a community in which to test my inner leadings, to help me discern whether the voice I’m hearing is coming from the light or the darkness I carry within me.

> **The community I need is not one that gives me answers,** but one that knows how to help me listen more and more deeply to “the inner teacher.” I need a community that helps me discern true from false and right from wrong, knowing that the only answers with staying power for my life are those that come from within, not those imposed from without.

**III. Here’s the rub: in spite of fifty years of Quaker-style** listening, discernment and formation in the [theory and practice of nonviolence](https://substack.com/redirect/c7ee0426-9186-4d1e-948a-360cd7c899bc?j=eyJ1IjoiMW91ejZvIn0.xCUUX-G1mqOe_p0uPEybRD6sQoY3OtX97OlEZbEJxWg), I come close to blowing sky-high whenever I learn about the latest MAGA/Project 2025 crime against humanity and the common good.

> **Fortunately, I don’t suffer from scattergun anger,** the way POTUS does. My failure to win a Nobel Peace Prize has not led me to kick my dog or burn down a house, let alone threaten war against a sovereign people. My anger is triggered when I see people like POTUS and his minions use their power and wealth in cruel and corrupt ways that hurt folks who are [vulnerable](https://substack.com/redirect/1dcea8f1-bb79-463d-b8ef-181568e4791d?j=eyJ1IjoiMW91ejZvIn0.xCUUX-G1mqOe_p0uPEybRD6sQoY3OtX97OlEZbEJxWg)—folks who need and deserve a helping hand from those of us who have more than we need.

> **My problem runs deep enough that a few years ago,** a conservative friend with whom I was having a series of “spirited” political discussions gave me a T-shirt that reads, “One Mean Quaker.” I wear those scarlet letters with un-Quakerly pride.

**IV. Does anger have a role to play in the life of someone** who aspires to nonviolence? My answer is a simple yes—and I feel no need for therapy to “fix my problem.” Truth be told, if I _didn’t_ feel anger toward POTUS and MAGA, I’d get to a therapist ASAP. Becoming emotionally numb to evil leads to the death of the human heart, and I intend to keep mine alive.

> **By their own admission, one of the MAGA machine’s** most potent weapons is “flooding the zone with s \_ \_ t.” By engulfing us in a [deluge of](https://substack.com/redirect/0167435a-c13c-40cd-8623-1cbf5c3cf75f?j=eyJ1IjoiMW91ejZvIn0.xCUUX-G1mqOe_p0uPEybRD6sQoY3OtX97OlEZbEJxWg) _[merde](https://substack.com/redirect/0167435a-c13c-40cd-8623-1cbf5c3cf75f?j=eyJ1IjoiMW91ejZvIn0.xCUUX-G1mqOe_p0uPEybRD6sQoY3OtX97OlEZbEJxWg)_, they hope to wear us down to a point where we stop resisting. Anger can serves as a source of energy for the work we need to do, and a source of stamina to help us carry on in hard times. _Everything depends on our capacity to harness our anger in the service of life-giving actions instead of harboring it in ways that bring us down._

> **I love the old saw that** “not forgiving is like drinking rat poison and waiting for the rat to die.” But forgiveness, I’ve discovered, is not always mine to give, especially in relation to people who have long histories of malicious acts they refuse to acknowledge, let alone atone for. Sometimes I have to pass the forgiveness baton to higher powers, as Iris DeMent does in her [tragicomic song](https://substack.com/redirect/55828432-74e4-4adb-b7cd-bf8905f6c02e?j=eyJ1IjoiMW91ejZvIn0.xCUUX-G1mqOe_p0uPEybRD6sQoY3OtX97OlEZbEJxWg) about betrayal: “God may forgive you, but I won’t. Jesus may love you, but I don’t.”

**V. For me, the spiritual life is not about** escaping the world’s trouble by flying up, up and away. It’s about finding the right balance of grounding and passion to stay in the fray. Anger—rightly understood, harnessed and deployed—is an emotion that keeps me grounded in reality while giving me the energy to pursue possibility, no matter how remote.

> **Anger repressed can contribute** to psychological breakdowns like depression, and chronic anger can contribute to bodily breakdowns like high blood pressure and heart disease. As one who has some experience with clinical depression, I have no desire to revisit that deadly state of mind.

> **But when I get angry about the way** people with power and wealth treat folks who must scramble daily simply to stay alive, it feels as if I have tapped into a vital stream of life—a stream that connects us to each other in the web of being, the ecosystem of all life. What hurts one of us hurts all of us, and feeling the hurt viscerally reassures me that I’m still in the web, holding and being held.

**VI. Day by day, as I take [concrete actions](https://substack.com/redirect/5e548902-a952-4b70-b92f-75f3c49f0f3d?j=eyJ1IjoiMW91ejZvIn0.xCUUX-G1mqOe_p0uPEybRD6sQoY3OtX97OlEZbEJxWg)** that harness my anger in service of love, truth and justice, I take time to listen to voices that remind me of life’s infinite possibilities.

> **I listen to nature, to music, to the poets,** to trusted teachers, to people whom I regard as exemplars in leading effective nonviolent lives. I also continue to listen in solitude and community for what is in and out of balance within me.

> **I’m not ready for “The Peaceable Kingdom” yet,** not ready to lie down with predators still red in tooth and claw who continue to devour the vulnerable. Still, I want to live in a spirit that helps lay the ground on which we can take a step closer to Martin Luther King Jr.’s “[Beloved Community](https://substack.com/redirect/910eab07-c92b-474f-b5aa-7dc75daf88d0?j=eyJ1IjoiMW91ejZvIn0.xCUUX-G1mqOe_p0uPEybRD6sQoY3OtX97OlEZbEJxWg).”

> **At the same time, I like to keep my options open,** taking my cues from an old Quaker story: A Quaker farmer owned a mule that refused to do a lick of work. Having run out of patience, the farmer went to the barn one morning, lifted the mule’s ear and calmly delivered a message: “Friend mule, Thee knows I am a Quaker. Thee knows I will not yell or curse at Thee. Thee knows I will not throw rocks at Thee, or kick Thee, or hit Thee with a stick. But what Thee does not know is that I might sell Thee to the Baptist farmer down the road who will beat the living daylights out of Thee.” Where there’s a will, there’s a way…

Yes, I watch the Green Bay Packers play. Here in Wisconsin, that’s called patriotism.
