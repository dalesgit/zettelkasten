Morning affronts the day after a death.
"George is gone" is an evening perception.
The next day there is only absence,
no presently self-awareness to say,
"I am."

Morning is a scandal to the emptiness of our
mourning.

Morning slips almost silently above the ocean level,
defying the gravity that keeps us bound
to the day at hand, the plow in hand, the task for doing,
the phantasm of ought.

Morning is a promise to our mourning
as well as a blast in the face of our sorrow 
at letting go. 

Morning slaps at the deep fatigue
that dwells
when we are left alone
suddenly
as always
death.
