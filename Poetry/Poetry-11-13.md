What feeds me?, asked [[MP]].
This re our church does not. 
I wrote yesterday about that. 
I said at lunch I’m not typical. 
Or maybe more than I admit. 
As mass was underway I returned to the question. 
“Judy. Sarah.” 
They both proclaimed, “This is home!”
The occasional joy of the under class?
 Having been given the grace to have touched the humble. 
That feeds me. 

Then in the midst of mass
I thought of another source of food. 
Bread and wine at the Eucharist 
(even when from an incompetent minister and bread that resembles fish food
And wine more like a finger bath
Than the common cup. 

Anawim. Anamnesis. 
The poor and the presence. 
These feed me.

> Anawim (pronounced ann-a-weem or ah-nah-weem) is a Hebrew word from the Old Testament that describes the "poor ones" who remained faithful to God during times of difficulty. It is derived from the Hebrew root meaning "bowed down" or "humble," and refers to those who are poor, afflicted, lowly, or meek, both in a socio-economic and spiritual sense. The term encompasses not only the economically destitute but also the socially marginalized—such as widows, orphans, the sick, and the outcast—who place their total trust in God rather than in their own strength or worldly power. The anawim are often described as the "faithful remnant" who, through their humility and dependence on God, inherit the earth and are promised divine protection and deliverance.

**Anamnesis** (from the [Attic Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attic_Greek "Attic Greek")word ἀνάμνησις, lit. 'reminiscence' or 'memorial sacrifice')[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anamnesis_\(Christianity\)#cite_note-1) is a [liturgical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgical "Liturgical")statement in [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") in which the [Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Church "Christian Church") refers to the [memorial character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorialism "Memorialism") of the [Eucharist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucharist "Eucharist") or to the [Passion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passion_\(Christianity\) "Passion (Christianity)"), [Resurrection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resurrection_of_Jesus "Resurrection of Jesus") and [Ascension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascension_of_Jesus_Christ "Ascension of Jesus Christ")of [Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus "Jesus"). It has its origin in Jesus' words at the [Last Supper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Supper "Last Supper"), "Do this in memory of me" ("τοῦτο ποιεῖτε εἰς τὴν ἐμὴν _ἀνάμνησιν_"), ([Luke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Luke "Gospel of Luke") 22:19, [1 Corinthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_Corinthians "1 Corinthians") 11:24-25).[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anamnesis_\(Christianity\)#cite_note-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anamnesis_\(Christianity\)#cite_note-3)




Anamnesis is also a key concept in the liturgical theology: in worship, Christians recall God's saving deeds.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anamnesis_\(Christianity\)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKunzler200161-4) This memorial aspect is not simply a passive process but one by which the Christian can actually enter into the [Paschal mystery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paschal_mystery "Paschal mystery").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anamnesis_\(Christianity\)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEsp%C3%ADn200750-5)
