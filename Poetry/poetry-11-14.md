I dreamed I was problem solving,
as on a loop, over and over.
It felt modestly successful, satisfying.
I felt useful not Sisyphean.

I slip into gender-type stereotypes
without even being aware of it.
Humans the tool makers.
Men the problem-solvers.
Then I wonder. 

I wonder whether usefulness is over-priced?
Is it the tool-maker species that has created our death-spiral?
Where did I first meet the critique of using fellow persons on our journey.
Kant? Augustine? Plato?

Levels of love or friendship?
Clearly to use another is baser than enjoying their presence.
All of use other people to some extent,
All the time, in all kinds of circumstances,
From high like an admission's counselor or human resources "officer"
to low like a cashier at Starbucks.®

Kant, ["used as means to end"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/persons-means/)
Plato, ["ladder of love"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/plato-friendship/)
Bernard, ["levels of love"](https://renovarGe.org/articles/four-degrees-of-love.md)
Biblical, ["from eros to agape"](https://iep.utm.edu/love/)

At this moment I'm most alert to usefulness
as a way of measuring a person's character.

_Samuel Johnson_ "The true measure of a man is how he treats someone who can do him absolutely no good.

Oh, I may be handy and a problem-solver, but if I have not love …
